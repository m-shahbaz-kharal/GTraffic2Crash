The choice that shapes everything else

Why forecast the grid instead of classifying a crash

A crash label is one bit at one instant. It is rare, it arrives too late to act on, and a model trained on it learns a shortcut — two blobs are close together — rather than dynamics.

A forecast is dense supervision: every cell, at every future step. And the representation is chosen so that a collision has an unmistakable signature. Each cell holds the fraction of its area covered by a vehicle footprint, not a binary flag, so two vehicles occupying the same ground saturate that cell. Soft coverage also keeps sub-metre position on a 4 m grid: a car straddling a boundary splits its area between the two cells in proportion, which is what makes velocity observable across frames even when a vehicle moves less than one cell.

Rotated vehicle rectangle against axis-aligned cell, by convex-polygon clipping. Validated against closed-form geometry and against the upstream project's own grids.

Throughput, congestion and collision rate measured before committing to a data plan. Every number below came from a probe, not an assumption.

50 hours of traffic

400 episodes across 40 CARLA servers. Raw vehicle state at 10 Hz, so resolution and time step stay revisable.

Per-class coverage and flow, stored sparsely at 1.6 KB a frame and densified in the loader.

Against the baselines

Steps 03–04

The data, and what it looks like

The map is given to the network as constant input channels. It is free information that would otherwise cost capacity to rediscover: 28 % of cells touch road and hold about 80 % of all vehicle mass.

Half the town is standing still

This is the number that says whether the task is worth doing at all.

Half the town is stopped at any moment, which is what urban traffic with signals looks like, and is why persistence is a strong baseline. It is also what makes the problem interesting rather than degenerate: the model's real job is to tell the vehicle that will sit still for five seconds from the one that will travel thirty-six metres — a distinction that cannot be made from a single frame. It needs the queue, the signal phase implied by the traffic's own behaviour, and the road ahead.

Step 05

The network

Two measured results from the literature drove the shape, not taste.

Attention along one axis at a time

Earthformer ablates seven ways of factorising attention over a space-time grid under identical conditions. Attending to time, then height, then width — plus a handful of global vectors to restore long-range mixing — wins on every metric on both benchmarks and is among the cheapest. Joint space-time cuboids and Video-Swin windows are strictly dominated.

No recurrence

On low-frequency grid data — traffic flow and weather, the closest public analogues — the OpenSTL benchmark finds that predicting every future frame in one parallel pass matches recurrent rollout at a twenty-sixth of the compute, and sidesteps autoregressive drift entirely. Town traffic at 2 Hz is low-frequency.

The objective

Most of the conventional choices are wrong for this target

The target is soft. A cell holding 0.4 is not a weak positive; it is an exact measurement of area. That single fact rules out most of what the occupancy literature reaches for.

Ruled outWhy, specifically
Soft Dice, soft IoU, Tversky Provably not minimised at the truth for fractional targets. Reproduced here: with a target of 0.400, linear soft Dice is minimised at 1.000, while soft cross-entropy is minimised at 0.400. Every non-zero cell in this grid is fractional, so soft Dice would inflate all of them.
Lovász-softmax The convex closure of a set function over misclassified pixel sets — undefined when the ground truth is 0.4. Applying it needs thresholding, which destroys the sub-cell information that is the entire point of soft coverage.
Focal loss as the primary term Not strictly proper for any γ > 0; its minimiser is not the true posterior and the bias direction is under-confidence, which here means quietly losing vehicles.

So the core is a plain soft cross-entropy

It is minimised exactly at p = y, defined for fractional targets without modification, and decomposable over cells — so its gradient scale does not swing with how many vehicles happen to be in the frame, as every Dice-family loss does.

The usual objection is that 98 % empty cells will swamp the gradient. That bites only at initialisation, where a network predicting 0.5 everywhere has twenty thousand empty cells outvoting four hundred occupied ones. The fix is the one Lin et al. prescribed for exactly this regime: initialise the output bias to the prior. After that, an empty cell the network already calls empty has gradient p − y, which is near zero.

Three more terms carry what a per-cell loss cannot see: flow (what the motion is, not just where the mass is), flow-trace (that mass moves rather than teleporting), and count (that the number of vehicles is right).

What went wrong

Three bugs, none visible from the total loss

All three were found by one test: train on a single batch and watch whether the network can fit what it is shown. The total loss fell steadily throughout — on the strength of the flow term — while the occupancy term had not moved since initialisation.

Silent

The flow-trace term was warping the prediction

Symptom
Occupancy cross-entropy sat at 0.19264 for 120 steps and 0.1613 for 400. Soft-IoU never left persistence.
Cause
Warping the predicted occupancy makes the term a second, much louder opinion about occupancy. Early in training the predicted flow is meaningless, so that opinion points nowhere.
Evidence
The gradient it put on the occupancy head was 5.76e-2 against the occupancy term's own 1.10e-3 — fifty times larger.
Fix
Warp the observed occupancy instead. The term keeps its meaning — your flow must explain the motion that actually happened — while its gradient is confined to the flow head.
Scale

The count term was a sum where everything else was a mean

Symptom
Total mass pinned; BIAS read exactly 1.000 while the forecast stayed at persistence.
Cause
The cross-entropy is a mean over ~1.7 million weighted cells, so its gradient on one cell is of order 1/1.7e6. The count term is a sum over the same cells, so its gradient on a cell is of order 1/(vehicle count) — about a thousand times larger. At any weight that makes the two comparable in value, the count term simply wins.
Fix
Weight chosen by matching the two gradients at an occupied cell, which puts it near 1e-3 rather than 0.5.
Interference

Occupancy and flow shared a head body

Symptom
Even with the other two fixed, occupancy learned at a third of its solo rate.
Cause
The flow gradient dominated the features both heads read from.
Fix
Separate fusion bodies — two extra 3×3 convolutions, 150k parameters. The transformer trunk stays shared, which is where multi-task transfer actually lives.

The fixed objective now learns occupancy slightly faster than the occupancy term on its own — which is what auxiliary tasks are supposed to do, and the opposite of what they were doing before. Worth noting what the loss is not able to reach: cross-entropy against a soft target bottoms out at the target's entropy, not at zero. On a real batch that floor is 0.055 against persistence's 0.201, so 73 % of the gap is genuinely reducible — a frozen term was a real failure, not an artefact.

Three things follow. The bar is 0.429. Constant velocity with estimated flow is worse than doing nothing, because dense optical flow on a near-binary sparse grid is unreliable — so the oracle version is the honest one to beat. And oracle constant velocity, far better at half a second, is worse than persistence at five (0.307 against 0.312): repeated warping spreads mass, and vehicles do not travel in straight lines for five seconds.

That crossover is the shape of the problem. Short horizons reward motion modelling; long horizons reward knowing where the roads go and where the traffic will stop.

Measurement

Why every table here carries BIAS

A deterministic forecaster can win a thresholded overlap score by hedging — predicting less area than really exists, so misses and false alarms trade favourably. A forecast holding only 0.3× the true mass still scores F1 0.843; only BIAS exposes it, at 0.303. Nothing is averaged over the horizon either: a single number hides how fast quality decays with lead time, and every model in this literature decays.

On what these numbers are. Every figure here comes from each run's own validation, on episodes it never trained on -- the split is by episode, never by window, because windows from one episode overlap heavily and share the same vehicles. A standalone evaluation of the pilot against a re-derived split was run and deliberately discarded: the pilot trained on 129 episodes while 355 were on disk by then, so the "held-out" set overlapped its training data. Those numbers looked better and meant nothing. Runs now record their exact split so this cannot happen again.

The question the project exists for

Does the forecast make a crash visible before it happens?

Measured, not trained for. No term in the loss singles collisions out; if the forecast shows a crash coming, it is because forecasting the town well is enough.

For every held-out window whose future contains a real collision, the impact cell and the step it happens at are known, so the forecast's occupancy at that spot can be tracked as impact approaches. That number alone would prove nothing — a model predicting high occupancy everywhere would score well — so it is measured against a matched control site: a cell occupied in the last observed frame, well away from the impact. The ratio is what matters.

The forecast also tracks the truth at the impact site closely (0.0800 against 0.0807 two seconds out), so it is predicting the convergence rather than guessing.

Honest limits

What this does not yet establish

One town. Town05 is the town the upstream camera model was built for, so it is the right first target, but nothing here says the model transfers. That is a scaling axis, not a correctness question.

The forecast will be diffuse. Every deterministic forecaster in this literature is. The response is to measure it — BIAS and a pooling sweep are in every table — rather than hide it. The documented next step is a cascade: a deterministic backbone with a generative refinement stage, which beats both pure approaches. Not a switch to pure diffusion, which collapses on grid-level skill.

Crash anticipation is measured, not trained for. Forecast quality is reported separately on windows whose future contains a real collision, but no term in the loss singles them out. Whether the forecast makes an imminent collision legible is a question this answers rather than one it optimises.

Built on CARLA 0.9.15, Town05. Grid geometry, coordinate conventions and the mass convention are shared with the upstream camera-to-bird's-eye model, so the two are interchangeable.
