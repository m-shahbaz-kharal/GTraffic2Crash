The choice that shapes everything else

Why forecast the grid instead of classifying a crash

A crash label is one bit at one instant. It is rare, it arrives too late to act on, and a model trained on it learns a shortcut — two blobs are close together — rather than dynamics.

A forecast is dense supervision: every cell, at every future step. And the representation is chosen so that a collision has an unmistakable signature. Each cell holds the fraction of its area covered by a vehicle footprint, not a binary flag, so two vehicles occupying the same ground saturate that cell. Soft coverage also keeps sub-metre position on a 4 m grid: a car straddling a boundary splits its area between the two cells in proportion, which is what makes velocity observable across frames even when a vehicle moves less than one cell.

Order of work

Each step produces something checkable before the next begins

01 / GEOMETRY

Exact rasteriser

Rotated vehicle rectangle against axis-aligned cell, by convex-polygon clipping. Validated against closed-form geometry and against the upstream project's own grids.

02 / PROBE

Measure the simulator

Throughput, congestion and collision rate measured before committing to a data plan. Every number below came from a probe, not an assumption.

03 / GENERATE

50 hours of traffic

400 episodes across 40 CARLA servers. Raw vehicle state at 10 Hz, so resolution and time step stay revisable.

04 / TENSORISE

Sparse rasters

Per-class coverage and flow, stored sparsely at 1.6 KB a frame and densified in the loader.

05 / TRAIN

Against the baselines

Persistence and constant velocity are scored on the same batches every validation epoch, so the only number reported is the gap.

What the simulator would actually give us

Six questions that decide the shape of the dataset, and none of them answerable from documentation.

How fast does Town05 run? 4.4× realtime at 60 vehicles, 1.1× at 200, 0.8× at 302 Fleet size set to 100–260; 40 parallel servers

Do Traffic-Manager vehicles ever crash? Never with default settings — 0 per minute Nominal episodes are a genuinely clean baseline
… with bad actors? 126 per simulated minute across 172 of 250 vehicles Behaviour profiles rewritten — see below
Is a finer physics timestep worth it? dt 0.025 s costs 30% throughput, changes nothing at a 0.5 s output step Simulator fixed at 20 Hz

The bad-actor result was the useful one. Telling vehicles to ignore_vehicles_percentage makes them drive through each other — 126 collisions a minute is carnage, not traffic. The profiles were rewritten to leave collision avoidance on and break the rules instead: running red lights, speeding with a short following gap, weaving between lanes. Crashes then happen for the reasons real crashes happen, at roughly one meaningful impact per simulated minute.
