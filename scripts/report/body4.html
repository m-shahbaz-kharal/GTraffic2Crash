The question the project exists for

Does the forecast make a crash visible before it happens?

Measured, not trained for. No term in the loss singles collisions out; if the forecast shows a crash coming, it is because forecasting the town well is enough.

For every held-out window whose future contains a real collision, the impact cell and the step it happens at are known, so the forecast's occupancy at that spot can be tracked as impact approaches. That number alone would prove nothing — a model predicting high occupancy everywhere would score well — so it is measured against a matched control site: a cell occupied in the last observed frame, well away from the impact. The ratio is what matters.

The forecast concentrates 1.6× more occupancy at the exact place a crash happens than at a matched control site, and the signal decays smoothly to nothing by four and a half seconds — an honest three-to-four second warning horizon rather than a claimed one. The forecast also tracks the truth at the impact site closely (0.0800 against 0.0807 two seconds out), so it is predicting the convergence rather than guessing.

Measured on the pilot against a re-derived split, so these numbers are indicative rather than a clean held-out result; the headline run records its split and will produce the definitive version. The analysis now records which kind of split it used alongside the numbers.

Honest limits

What this does not yet establish

One town. Town05 is the town the upstream camera model was built for, so it is the right first target, but nothing here says the model transfers. That is a scaling axis, not a correctness question.

The forecast will be diffuse. Every deterministic forecaster in this literature is. The response is to measure it — BIAS and a pooling sweep are in every table — rather than hide it. The documented next step is a cascade: a deterministic backbone with a generative refinement stage, which beats both pure approaches. Not a switch to pure diffusion, which collapses on grid-level skill.

Crash anticipation is measured, not trained for. Forecast quality is reported separately on windows whose future contains a real collision, but no term in the loss singles them out. Whether the forecast makes an imminent collision legible is a question this answers rather than one it optimises.

Built on CARLA 0.9.15, Town05. Grid geometry, coordinate conventions and the mass convention are shared with the upstream camera-to-bird's-eye model, so the two are interchangeable. Weights & Biases runs offline on this cluster; training also writes a JSON-lines validation log and a forecast video per epoch, so progress is readable without it.
